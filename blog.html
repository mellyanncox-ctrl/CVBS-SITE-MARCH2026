How to write a conference brief
that gets better venue options

Karen Jepson, CVBS
5 min read
March 2026
The quality of your venue shortlist is almost entirely determined by the quality of your brief. A vague brief produces vague options. A specific brief, even an incomplete one, produces exactly the right venues for your event.

After 35 years of receiving venue briefs, we can tell you that the best ones share a handful of qualities. They're specific about the non-negotiables, honest about the budget, and upfront about what delegates actually need, not just what the event is called on the calendar.

Here's what to include, why it matters, and what happens when we don't have it.

1. Event type, be specific

"Conference" tells us very little. "Annual staff conference for 120 people, 2 days, plenary and 4 breakout sessions, with a gala dinner on night one" tells us everything we need to start shortlisting. The more specific you are about the event's structure, the better we can match it to a venue's actual capabilities.

What to include:
Name of the event · Its purpose · Number of sessions · Whether there's a social program · Any theme or tone (formal / informal / residential / day only)

2. Delegate count, and who they are

Numbers matter for room capacity. But who the delegates are matters just as much. A conference for 80 senior executives has very different venue requirements to a training day for 80 frontline staff. We shortlist differently based on both.

3. Dates, flexible or fixed?

If your dates are fixed, tell us. If you have a window of two or three weeks, tell us that too, because flexibility significantly changes what we can access and what we can negotiate. Many excellent venues have premium availability within a flexible window that would be fully booked on your first preference date.

4. Budget, the number people don't like sharing

We understand the instinct to withhold budget. The reality is the opposite: without a budget, we present a broader range of options that takes longer for you to evaluate. With a budget, we shortlist precisely and negotiate harder within it.

Budget detailWhat we can do
Per head per day (DDR)Direct comparison across all shortlisted venues
Total event budgetWe work backwards to identify viable venues and inclusions
No budget givenWe present a wider range, takes longer to evaluate
"Best available rate"We negotiate hard, but without an anchor, harder to benchmark

5. The brief items people forget

1
Dietary requirements, not a given, but mention if you know. Large pharma conferences often have significant requirements.
2
AV requirements, do you have a presenter, a panel, breakout facilitators? This shapes the room setup and AV package.
3
Transport / accessibility, does the venue need to be near a train station? Airport? Parking for regional delegates?
4
What you don't want, if you've used a particular venue and it didn't work, tell us. It saves everyone time.
5
Vibe or tone, formal hotel vs unique or relaxed setting is a more useful filter than you'd think.

If you're not sure about some of these details yet, that's fine. Tell us what you do know and flag what's still being decided. We'll ask the right questions to fill in the gaps.
