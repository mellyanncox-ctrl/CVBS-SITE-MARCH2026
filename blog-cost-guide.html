The real cost of a conference venue in Australia in 2026

Karen Jepson, CVBS
7 min read
March 2026

One of the most common questions Karen and Anthony hear from new clients is: "What should we budget?" It's a reasonable question with an unreasonably complex answer, because the cost of a conference venue in Australia varies enormously depending on city, venue type, season, delegate count and what's included in the package.

This guide is designed to give you a realistic benchmark for 2026, not the theoretical rates you'll find on venue websites, but the numbers we see in actual briefs and negotiations.

"A $150 per person day delegate rate in the Hunter Valley is a very different proposition to a $150 per person day delegate rate in the Sydney CBD. Location changes everything."

What is a day delegate rate?

The day delegate rate (DDR) is the hospitality industry's standard unit for conference pricing. It typically includes room hire, morning tea, lunch, afternoon tea, AV equipment (screen, projector, microphone) and sometimes WiFi and parking. It does not usually include dinners, accommodation, off-site activities or premium AV.

The advantage of DDR pricing is that it bundles costs into a single comparable number. The catch is that DDR packages vary widely in what they actually include, so you need to read the fine print.

Residential conference costs

If your event runs over one or more nights, you're looking at residential rates, which include accommodation in addition to the day delegate inclusions. In 2026, residential conference packages at 4-star regional properties typically run:

  • One night residential (1 night, 2 days): $280–$420pp at regional properties
  • Two night residential: $480–$700pp at 4-star regional, $600–$900pp at 5-star
  • City residential (CBD hotel): $350–$550pp per night all-in

What drives the price up

The biggest cost variables we see in briefs are:

Gala dinners

A formal gala dinner adds $80–$180pp on top of your day rate. This is often the single biggest line item in a conference budget. If you're trying to manage costs, an informal or shared-plate dinner format can halve this cost without sacrificing the experience.

AV requirements

Standard AV (screen, projector, microphone) is usually included in DDR packages. But if you need a large LED wall, simultaneous breakout rooms with full AV, live streaming or professional stage management, you can add $5,000–$30,000 to the total.

Breakout rooms

Some venues include breakout rooms in their DDR rate; others charge per room. At busy city venues, breakout room hire can add $400–$1,200 per room per day. Always clarify this upfront.

Minimum numbers

Most venues have minimum spend or minimum delegate numbers. A venue that prices at $130pp with a 50-person minimum is a very different proposition to one with no minimum.

Seasonal pricing

Australian conference venues have peak and shoulder seasons that affect pricing significantly:

  • Peak (May–August): Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane are most in demand. Expect premium pricing and limited availability at popular venues.
  • Shoulder (March–April, September–October): Good availability at most venues. Negotiation is easier. Some venues will add complimentary inclusions to secure bookings.
  • Off-peak (November–February): January is very slow for corporate events. December is popular for end-of-year events. February can offer good value.

How to get better rates

A few things that consistently help in negotiating conference rates:

  • Flexible dates: Even a week's flexibility dramatically increases your negotiating position. If you can shift by three to four weeks, you can often improve your rate by 10–20%.
  • Commitment to food and beverage: Venues make margin on F&B. If you signal you'll run a full dinner and all-day catering, they'll often discount room hire.
  • Multi-year commitment: If this is a recurring event, committing to a second year in advance gives you significant leverage.
  • Booking through CVBS: The volume we place gives us negotiating access and relationships that individual bookers rarely have.

The bottom line

Budget early, build in contingency (10–15% is sensible), and be honest about your number when you submit a brief. A realistic budget brief produces better shortlist options than a vague one.
